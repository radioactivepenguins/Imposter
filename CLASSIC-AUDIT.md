# Classic word bank audit and revision

Reviewed October 19, 2026 for casual American college groups, approximately ages 18–25. This edits the existing site; it does not rebuild it.

**155 entries were replaced with different words; 11 labels were clarified or corrected, for 166 changed word labels total. 1,021 hint fields changed, including 858 changes on retained words.** The other hint changes accompany substitutions or label changes. All 1,080 word/hint combinations were reviewed.

## Editorial approach

Kept familiar food, cultural traditions, places, and people from around the world. Replaced niche car models, casino games, formal meme names, technical finance terms, unfamiliar platforms, several franchise references, and regional chains. Hints use everyday language and shared associations within the category. Many intentionally overlap: the Imposter sees the category and should have several plausible answers. Hint difficulty and recognizability remain editorial judgments, rather than guarantees for every group.

## Categories substantially revised

- Cards & Party Games: replaced eight requested games and additional niche titles with familiar card and party games.
- Cars & Vehicles: combines recognizable models with familiar vehicle types.
- Casino & Gambling: familiar objects, people, prizes, and situations replace specialist games.
- Memes: readable internet phrases and recognizable references replace older formal template names.
- Apps & Social Media: familiar everyday apps replace lesser-known platforms.
- Restaurants & Dining: familiar chains and common dining settings replace niche chains.
- Board Games, Video Games, Toys, and Money & Finance also received substantial substitutions.
- Every category received a hint review; sports, people, nature, stores, countries, and superheroes received extensive hint revisions.

## Saved games and preserved behavior

Only three runtime files changed: data/classicWords.js, engine.js, and script.js. engine.js maps old category names and clarified word names to their new labels in saved history. script.js uses that mapping when restoring category settings after refresh. Category selection, word selection, question selection, and role-assignment algorithms otherwise retain the original behavior.

index.html, style.css, all icons, the manifest, .nojekyll, and the full Question database are byte-for-byte identical to the original ZIP. The existing page shell is included and loads the revised database. Question rounds match the original under identical seeded random inputs.

## Remaining borderline familiarity

- Cards and board games: Crazy Eights, Hearts (Card Game), Spades, Spoons, What Do You Meme?, Catan, Ticket to Ride, Codenames, Trivial Pursuit, and Boggle. These are recognizable named games for many groups, but knowledge varies. No detailed rules are needed to participate.
- Memes: Skibidi Toilet, Gigachad, Delulu, NPC, Rizz, and the retained Distracted Boyfriend/Woman Yelling at a Cat references. Internet exposure varies; descriptive labels and ordinary hints help.
- Chains: In-N-Out, Raising Cane's, Waffle House, Cracker Barrel, and Red Robin. These remain regional to varying degrees despite broader name recognition.
- People and fiction: Giannis Antetokounmpo, Nikola Jokić, Naomi Osaka, SZA, Katniss Everdeen, Scarlet Witch, and Vision (Marvel) may be less familiar to nonfans. They retain broad sports/music/movie exposure and accessible hints.
- Foods and traditions such as Horchata, Falafel, Diwali, and Eid al-Fitr were retained deliberately; international origin alone was not grounds for removal.

These are the main candidates for later refinement from actual group play; the goal is improved accessibility without flattening cultural or category variety.
